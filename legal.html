1. Who these terms are between

Laneway ("we", "us") is a trip planning and navigation service operated by an independent developer based in Alberta, Canada. "You" are the person using the Laneway app or the Laneway service. By using either, you agree to these terms. The app itself is licensed to you under the End User Licence Agreement; your data is handled as described in the Privacy Policy.

2. What Laneway is

Laneway plans multi-modal trips, including the legs that ordinary mapping apps cannot see, and navigates you along them. Plans are produced with the help of AI models and then checked against sources: official transit feeds, operator pages, and other published data. Every factual claim in a plan carries its source and the time it was fetched.

3. What verified means, and what it does not

When Laneway marks something verified, that means the claim was traced to a quoted span of a named source at a stated time. It does not mean the claim is guaranteed to be true when you travel. Schedules change, shuttles stop running, feeds go stale, and sources are sometimes wrong. Anything Laneway could not verify is marked unverified rather than hidden, and an unverified leg is exactly what it says: a best guess you should confirm yourself, with the operator if it matters.

4. Travel decisions are yours

Laneway is information, not authority.

  • Follow posted signage, staff instructions, and local law when they disagree with the app. They win, every time.
  • Do not interact with the app while driving or cycling beyond what the law and common sense allow. Set up the trip before you move.
  • For anything with a real cost of failure, such as a last train, a border crossing, a flight connection, or a medical need, confirm the critical leg with the operator before relying on it.
  • You are responsible for your own safety and for the decision to take any route the app proposes.

You are responsible for keeping your device secure. You can sign out a device from Settings, which revokes that device's session without touching your others. 6. Fair use and metering

Privacy Policy Effective Wednesday Sep 2, 2026

What is collected, what is not, and where it goes.

1. The short version

  • Your places, saved trips, and preferences live on your phone.
  • When you plan a trip, the request is sent to a Laneway server and processed with the help of the third-party services named below.
  • Accounts are anonymous by default. Sign in with Apple is optional.
  • No ads, no analytics SDKs, no tracking across apps, and your data is never sold.

2. What stays on your phone

The app stores on the device, and only on the device: your Home and Work addresses, saved trips, recent destinations, mode preferences, and app settings. Saved trips are indexed with Spotlight so you can search them; that index also stays on the phone. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

3. What is sent when you plan a trip

A plan request contains what a plan needs: the origin and destination as you described them, the time window, your mode preferences and constraints, and, when you use your current location as a starting point, that location's coordinates. While navigating, guidance runs on the device; your live position is not streamed to the server.

4. What the server keeps

  • Your account: a random identifier and its creation time.
  • Sessions: one per signed-in device, stored as a cryptographic hash of the token, never the token itself.
  • Usage records: when a plan was made, what it cost to produce, and which trip it belonged to. This is how fair-use metering works.
  • Trip results, for the time it takes you to fetch them.
  • Ordinary server logs, kept briefly for debugging and abuse prevention.

5. Sign in with Apple

If you choose to sign in, Apple gives us a stable anonymous identifier for your Apple account, and, if you agree to share it, an email address, which may be Apple's private relay address rather than your real one. We store both. They are used to let you reclaim your account on a new phone, and for nothing else today. If you never sign in, we never have them.

6. Services that process your requests

  • Anthropic processes the text of your trip request to draft and check the plan.
  • Google routing services compute road and transit legs, when enabled.
  • Apple services on your device handle maps, geocoding, and address suggestions; those queries go to Apple from your phone under Apple's privacy terms.

Each processes only what a plan needs, under its own terms.

7. Cached public data

The server caches public data it fetched to verify plans: transit feed summaries and copies of public web pages. This cache is about the world, not about you; it holds no personal data.

8. What we do not do

No advertising, no third-party analytics, no data brokers, no selling or renting personal information, no tracking you across other apps or websites, and no reading anything on your phone beyond what the app needs to plan and navigate.

9. How long things are kept

Cached public data expires on its own schedule, from hours to days. Usage records are kept while your account exists, because they are what the metering stands on. Server logs rotate out on the order of weeks. When your account is deleted, its records go with it.

10. Security

Session tokens are stored only as hashes. Connections to a Laneway server over the public internet are encrypted with HTTPS. When the app finds a Laneway engine on your own local network, that traffic stays inside your network and is not encrypted in transit; that mode exists for running your own engine at home.

11. Children

Laneway is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their information.

12. Your rights

Canadian privacy law, PIPEDA, applies. You can ask what we hold about you, ask for corrections, withdraw consent, and ask for deletion, by contacting us (section 14). You can also complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

13. Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes, and the app will point at the new version. A change that collects more than this version describes will be called out plainly, not slipped in.
